## Findings

### High: observed_updates has the wrong meaning for PPO

In the reviewed `pipeline_plugins/rl_pipeline.py::_observed_work`, the docstring
says `_n_updates` advances per gradient update. In locally installed SB3 2.9.0,
`PPO.train` increments it once per epoch, outside the minibatch loop; the optimizer
steps inside that loop. A counter cannot have the same asserted meaning across
algorithms merely because they expose a same-named private field.

An independent CPU-only unit probe on CartPole with n_steps=256, batch_size=64,
n_epochs=10 and learn(64) measured:

| requested | environment transitions | SB3 _n_updates | optimizer.step calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 256 | 10 | 40 |

This is a unit check of library semantics, not a new project experiment or evidence
that the historical production run made exactly 40 optimizer calls. Its first shell
invocation had an indentation typo and did not execute; the corrected invocation
completed successfully. The reproducible probe is linked below.

### High: a learn target is not a hard resource ceiling

The explanation of 256 steps is supported: on-policy learn checks its target around
whole rollouts. For a fresh model, uninterrupted run and N environments, expected
collection is ceil(T/(n_steps*N))*(n_steps*N). Callbacks, resumed models and early
termination require separate treatment. Official [PPO documentation](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html)
defines n_steps per environment and n_epochs as optimization epochs.

Read-only PostgreSQL queries verified the recorded values for production runs:
prod-12: requested 64, observed 10240, updates 400;
prod-13: requested 64, observed 256, updates 10.
Recording both is more honest than substituting the budget, but it does not prove
a hard limit was enforced. The previous order specified requested steps; no new
retroactive assertion of an authorized observed ceiling is made here. Future
bounded acceptance must explicitly distinguish the two and enforce its ceiling.
